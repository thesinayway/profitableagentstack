# How real companies use AI for GTM (sourced results)

by [Limor Sinay](https://profitableagentstack.com/advisory), Profitable Agent Stack | [Artificial Intelligence (AI)](https://profitableagentstack.com/articles/category/ai), [AI for GTM](https://profitableagentstack.com/articles/category/ai-for-gtm), [Blog Posts](https://profitableagentstack.com/articles/category/blog-posts)

Named companies have published what happened after they put AI into go-to-market, and the pattern is consistent: AI scales a motion that already works, and it takes daily human training. SaaStr’s first-party posts say agents first-touch-sourced $4.8 million in added pipeline and $2.4 million in closed-won revenue after eight months, with 15–20 hours of management a week from each of two leaders. Vendor-published stories from monday.com, Payhawk, Pump.co and Asana report time savings and pipeline or booking gains, each cited to the original page below. This is not advice; verify with the sources, and treat vendor case studies as the vendor’s account.

**Direct answer:** The useful “AI for GTM” evidence is not a generic leaderboard. It is a small set of named companies describing a specific motion, the tools they used, and a result, with a link you can open. First-party posts (the company writing about itself) are stronger than vendor case studies. Vendor case studies can still be useful if you read them as the vendor’s account and follow the original link. None of the numbers below were invented for this page.

## SaaStr: first-party, a small team running many agents

SaaStr is the closest public write-up we have of a tiny team running AI across go-to-market and publishing the numbers.

In *6 Months of AI SDRs: What’s Worked, How They Brought In $1M+ in 90 Days, and the Real Data Everyone’s Asking For* ([SaaStr, Jason Lemkin](https://www.saastr.com/6-months-of-ai-sdrs-whats-worked-how-they-brought-in-1m-in-90-days-and-the-real-data-everyones-asking-for/)), Lemkin and Chief AI Officer Amelia Lerutte wrote that they were running five specialized agents and that:

- Outbound agents (they say “primarily [Artisan](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/ai-sdr#artisan)”) sent 19,847 messages in six months, with a 6.7% overall response rate and a 4% positive response rate.
- They compare that with 3,000 emails per month from AI versus 75–285 per month from previous human reps.
- An inbound agent ([Qualified](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/ai-sdr#qualified)), added later, was credited with $1M+ in closed revenue in 90 days, $2.5M+ in pipeline from agent-booked meetings, 100+ meetings, and 70% of October’s closed-won deals.
- 20% of event ticket revenue “now comes from AI agents selling directly.”
- They say each of them spends 15–20 hours a week managing the agents, and they budget $50–100K per specialized platform per year.

A later first-party update, *What We Actually Learned Deploying 20+ AI Agents Across Our Entire Go-To-Market (8 Months In)* ([SaaStr, Jason Lemkin](https://www.saastr.com/what-we-actually-learned-deploying-20-ai-agents-across-our-entire-go-to-market-8-months-in/)), raises the aperture to 20+ agents “with just 3 humans and a dog” and reports:

- $4.8 million in additional pipeline first-touch sourced from an agent
- $2.4 million in closed-won revenue first-touch sourced from an agent
- deal volume more than doubled, win rates nearly doubled
- more than 60,000 AI-generated emails on the sales side
- the same 15–20 hours a week of maintenance, from each of Lemkin and Lerutte
- the results were “additive” and did not, in their telling, cannibalize existing inbound

That later post also describes stitching [Clay](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/enrichment#clay), Artisan, Qualified and Salesforce Agentforce together, and says marketing-orchestration tools were less mature than sales agents, which is why they built some of their own marketing agents.

**What a small team can take from this:** SaaStr’s own advice is to put an agent on work that is already proven, or on work the team is simply not doing (ghosted leads, small follow-ups), and to plan for daily training. They say an AI SDR will not fix outbound that does not already work with humans. Contact-sales AI SDR tools belong in the “add later, when the motion already books meetings” bucket for most 2–5 person teams; see [AI SDR / agentic SDR](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/ai-sdr) and [when to skip intent data](https://profitableagentstack.com/articles/when-to-skip-intent-data).

## monday.com + Exa: vendor-published, list building for agents

Source: [monday.com x Exa case study](https://exa.ai/customers/monday), retrieved 24 November 2026 (America/New\_York). This is Exa’s customer story.

monday.com (Tel Aviv and global) describes building lead agents on [Exa](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/list-building#exa) Search so a rep can describe a segment in natural language and get structured companies and contacts from the live web. The page reports:

- 2.7 million enrichments completed “across the monday.com customer base”
- 50% less research time per SDR/AE, per prospecting cycle
- a “top 10 leads for the day” list waiting in monday CRM when a rep opens it

Daniel Lereya, Chief Product and Technology Officer, is quoted saying agents need “live intelligence” and that “Exa is where that comes from.” The same page is clear that a general-purpose LLM, on its own, did not produce usable emails, phone numbers and firmographics.

**What a small team can take from this:** “Ask an LLM to build my list” is not the same as a list-building or [enrichment](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/enrichment) tool with structured, sourced outputs. If you already have a list and need verified emails or mobiles, [FullEnrich](https://profitableagentstack.com/go/fullenrich) (affiliate) is the waterfall enrichment option we have an affiliate link for; [Hunter](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/enrichment#hunter) and [Clay](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/enrichment#clay) are the other published-price options in that use case. ## Payhawk + Clay: vendor-published, better account selection

Source: [How Payhawk rebuilt outbound with Clay and grew pipeline 3x](https://www.clay.com/customers/payhawk), retrieved 24 November 2026 (America/New\_York). This is Clay’s customer story. Payhawk is a London-headquartered spend management company.

Clay’s story, quoting CRO Gabriela Belnikolovska and VP of Revenue Operations Richard Standring, says Payhawk grew pipeline 3x by rebuilding an outbound motion that was already one of its strongest channels. The page says they:

- gave each BDR 100 high-fit named accounts
- expanded account scoring from three or four inputs to roughly 25 parameters
- added signals such as new CFO appointments and former Payhawk users joining a new company
- saw median deal size for outbound-sourced customers rise 70%
- saw 50% more reps hitting or exceeding quota each month
- saw partner-sourced leads reach win rates of up to 60% in some months

The through-line is not “we bought an AI SDR.” It is account selection and timing, with research written back into Salesforce and the sequencer so reps see *why* an account is on the list.

**What a small team can take from this:** Clay’s Launch plan is $167/mo on the annual-billing view ($185 monthly) in our file, last verified 2026-11-23 — a real cost for a 2–5 person team. ## Pump.co + Smartlead (and HeyReach): vendor-published, high-volume outbound

Source: [Pump.co Sent >130K Emails and Generated $200K in Revenue with Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/case-study/case-study-claire-hardesty), published 24 February 2026, updated 3 March 2026. This is Smartlead’s customer story, written by a Smartlead staff writer.

Pump.co is described as a Y Combinator-backed cloud cost optimization startup. The case study says founding GTM engineer Claire Hardesty built outbound from scratch with [Smartlead](https://profitableagentstack.com/go/smartlead) (affiliate) and that in the first month they:

- sent approximately 130,000 emails (ahead of a 100,000-a-month goal)
- generated “close to $200K in pipeline revenue attributed directly to Smartlead campaigns”

Note the body wording: **pipeline revenue**, not necessarily closed-won revenue. The headline on the same page says “$200K in Revenue.” We quote the body.

The story also says they housed nearly 800 email accounts in Smartlead, used [Clay](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/enrichment#clay) to route sends into local business hours across seven campaign types, and supplemented LinkedIn outreach with [HeyReach](https://profitableagentstack.com/go/heyreach) (affiliate). Copy was written in-house with Smartlead’s spintax features.

**What a small team can take from this:** 130,000 emails in a month and 800 mailboxes is a volume motion, not a default 2–5 person setup. The transferable idea is a clear sequencer plus enrichment plus, when it fits, a LinkedIn tool. ## Asana + Workato: vendor-published, an internal GTM engine

Source: [How Asana Built an AI Engine for GTM with Workato](https://www.workato.com/the-connector/asana-gtm-ai-engine/), Perri Bronson, 30 September 2026. This is Workato’s account of a talk by Maschal Malek, Product Manager for Enterprise AI at Asana.

Asana started with more than 100 possible use cases, then shipped four: competitive intelligence, pre-call planning, post-call planning, and account summaries. Reps had been spending about 10 hours a week researching across eight or more tools. The team of six, each spending 15 hours a week on top of their jobs, stitched together tools Asana already owned (including Salesforce, 6sense, ZoomInfo, Chorus, Databricks, OpenAI and Workato) rather than buying a new AI suite. Malek is quoted saying a generated account or prospect brief cost about 10 cents per record.

In the first quarter after a sales-kickoff launch to 260+ reps, the page reports:

- a 10% increase in bookings (annual recurring revenue closed)
- account research time cut from 10 hours a week to 1
- 2.3× higher email reply rates than previous outreach
- 86% adoption in that first quarter, later 100% after adjustments for some enterprise account structures

They later added a custom MCP server and a Claude plugin so reps could ask account questions without writing back to Salesforce.

**What a small team can take from this:** the win came from orchestrating data the team already had, starting with a narrow set of jobs, and keeping a human in the loop for drafts and feedback. You do not need Asana’s 260-rep rollout to borrow that sequence. ZoomInfo is in our directory as a contact-sales [list-building](https://profitableagentstack.com/use-cases/list-building#zoominfo) option for larger teams; it is not what we recommend first for a 2–5 person stack.

## A pattern you can use on a 2–5 person team

Across these write-ups, the companies that publish results do a few un-glamorous things first:

1. **They start with a motion that already works**, or with work nobody is doing. SaaStr says this explicitly.
2. **They keep a human trainer in the loop.** Two SaaStr leaders, and Asana’s six-person build team, all report serious weekly hours.
3. **They separate list building, enrichment, sending and (sometimes) AI SDR.** Pump.co used Smartlead + Clay + HeyReach. monday.com used Exa for live list and enrichment data inside its own CRM agents. Payhawk used Clay to decide *who* and *when*, not as a sender.
4. **They measure a specific outcome** (research hours, reply rate, pipeline, closed-won, bookings) and they say whether the figure is first-touch, influenced, pipeline or closed.
