Tool sprawl is quiet. A sequencer here, a second enrichment plan there, and a scheduler nobody opened since March. Each is small; together they are a real line on the budget.

## The rule

For every use case, pick **one** tool, write down why, and revisit it monthly. Our recommended stacks follow that rule with no paid overlap, and each row on a stack page says why that tool won.

## How to choose

1. **Name the job narrowly.** "Outbound email sequencing" is a job. "Sales automation" is not.
2. **Start from the free tier.** Several jobs are covered at $0 (for example Google Search Console for search data, or a free newsletter plan). Prove you need more before paying.
3. **Compare on value, not features.** We rank tools in each use case by [value score](/articles/how-we-score.html), then explain any pick that is not the top score.
4. **Count seats honestly.** Per-sender or per-seat pricing, such as LinkedIn tools, is where costs grow fastest as the team grows.

## When two tools in one stack is fine

Two tools are fine when they do **different** jobs. In our stacks, Search Console tracks search performance while Surfer optimizes content, and RB2B identifies site visitors while PostHog analyzes product usage. That is two jobs, not one job paid for twice.
